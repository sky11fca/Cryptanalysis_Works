"It is a truth universally acknowledged that aingle man in possession of a good fortune must be in want a wife. However known the feelings or views of such a man may be on his first entering a neighbourhood, this truth is so well fixed in the mind of the surrounding families that he is considered the rightful property of someone or other of their daughters. 'My dear Mr. Bennet,' said his lady to him one day, 'have you heard that Netherfield Park is let at last?' Mr. Bennet replied that he had not, bute she joined in at once with a fleet ofo explanations and excitement, declaring that it was taken by a young man of large fortune from the north of England, that he came down on Monday in a chaise and four to sen the place, and wass so much delighted with it that he agreed with Mr. Morris immediately that he should take possession before Michaelmas, and some of his servants were to be in the house by the end of next week."
